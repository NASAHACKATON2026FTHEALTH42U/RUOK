# Alerts and escalation

Alerts can be **automatic** (generated by the assessment) or **manual** (the user asks for help). Both use the same model, the same ladder and the same link.

## Ladder per environment
Defined in `environment.escalation_ladder`:
- Space: `self` → `crew` (crew / onboard doctor) → `ground` (flight surgeon).
- Hospital: `self` → `nurse` → `doctor`.
- Geriatrics: `self` → `caregiver` → `doctor` → `family`.

`self` and `crew` (and their Earth equivalents) **are resolved onboard**, without the link. The external levels (`ground`, `doctor`, `family`) go out through the **simulated link**.

The cut-off is set by `environment.external_from_level`: the index of the first external level in the ladder (2 in all 6 environments). The levels before it are onboard.

## Automatic alerts
1. Every `POST /measurements` (and every liveness change) recomputes the assessment.
2. A system in `warning` creates a `self`-level alert, with self-care recommendations.
3. It escalates on its own to the next level in these cases:
   - `critical`;
   - a `warning` that persists or worsens (trend) beyond the time set by the environment;
   - `no_contact` (see [offline-and-liveness.md](offline-and-liveness.md)).
4. **Deduplication.** There is at most one open alert per `member_id + system_id` (or per category). If the situation gets worse, the alert is updated (`updated`) instead of creating a new one.
5. If the situation returns to `normal` in a sustained way, the alert is proposed for resolution; it does not close on its own without leaving a trace.

## Manual alerts (SOS and notices to control)
- The **SOS** button is always visible and has no form: it warns that **the help alert will be sent unless cancelled within 3 s**, and then sends it on its own (`emergency`, to the next level of the environment's ladder). It also works without a session.
- For anything that is not an emergency there is **"Enviar aviso a control"** (Send notice to control), with a form. The category is chosen:
  - `symptom`;
  - `physical` (physical discomfort);
  - `psychological` ("Necesito hablar", I need to talk, which goes through a **confidential** channel);
  - `emergency`.
- The severity is also chosen and, optionally, a message.
- The destination can be chosen, **skipping levels** (for example, going straight to `ground`).
- Every automatic alert also has an **"Escalar"** (Escalate) button, which by default raises it to the next level.
- If SOS is pressed **without a connection to the onboard server**, the alert stays in the device outbox and the app shows: *"No enviado. Avisa a la tripulación de viva voz"* (Not sent. Tell the crew out loud), together with the autonomous protocol, which is cached.

## Follow-up for the member while their notice is open (FE-38)
An alert does not close on its own: whoever handles it closes it. Meanwhile, the member sees a follow-up message in the system detail, in the alert and after measuring (check-in, PVT):
- **«Estamos revisando tu caso»** (We are reviewing your case): the notice has reached its destination, or the destination has already seen it; follow the recommendations and, if things get worse, use SOS.
- **«Nos alegra ver que estás mejor»** (We're glad to see you're better): the system is better than when the notice was raised (e.g. from critical to attention), and whoever handles it will close the notice once they confirm it.

Only the member themselves sees this: a healthcare worker looking at their alerts does not.

## Lifecycle
```
open ──▶ escalated ──▶ acknowledged ──▶ resolved
  │           ▲
  └───────────┘ (automatic or manual escalation, can repeat)
```
Each step generates an `AlertEvent`: `created`, `updated`, `escalated`, `queued`, `delivered`, `acknowledged`, `message` or `resolved`. The front end shows it as a timeline.

## Simulated link with external control (`services/comm_link.py`)
The design is inspired by delay-tolerant networks (DTN), with **store-and-forward**:
- **outbox/inbox** queue persisted in SQLite. It survives restarts and **nothing is lost**.
- Each message leaves with `eta = sent_at + comm_delay_s` and becomes `delivered` when the demo clock reaches the ETA.
- Link states:
  - `online`: practically zero delay.
  - `delayed`: the environment's normal delay.
  - `los` (loss of signal): everything is queued and goes out when the link comes back.
- Replies from the ground come back through the same queue, with the same delay.
- The `/ground` console **only sees what has been delivered**.

### Escalation mode by level and delay
The rows with a delay only apply to **external** levels.

| `comm_delay_s` | `escalation.mode` | UX |
|---|---|---|
| Any, if the level is **onboard** (`level < external_from_level`) | `realtime`, `delay_s` 0 | The crew or the facility staff receive the alert instantly |
| < 5 s | `realtime` | "Contactando con el flight surgeon…" (Contacting the flight surgeon…) |
| ≥ 5 s | `delayed` | Countdown to the ETA |
| ≥ 60 s and severity ≥ `critical` | `autonomous` + `delayed` | Step-by-step **autonomous protocol** while the reply is on its way |

## Privacy
- `psychological` alerts and behavioral details are only seen by the responder of their channel (psychologist/BHP).
- The rest of the console sees "alerta conductual activa" (active behavioral alert) without details, unless the member has enabled `share_behavioral_details` or the severity is `emergency`.

## Demo scenarios
- `mars_sleep_bp`: several days of poor sleep and high blood pressure. An automatic alert is raised at `self` and then escalates to `ground` with a 14 min ETA and the autonomous protocol.
- SOS during `los`: the alert stays in the queue and is delivered when the link comes back (with `fast-forward`).
